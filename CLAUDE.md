# Resume pipeline house rules

These were saved preferences in claude.ai and applied automatically there. Claude Code only sees them from this file.

## Writing
- Never use em dashes in any output.

## Candidate resumes
- Profile template: "[title] with [N] years of experience in [focus area]. Highly experienced in [past fields covered]. Passionate about [a larger personal goal]. [traits important for the target role] who can [short description of capability]." Sentence 1 opens with the title itself, no soft-skill prefix ("Technical program manager with 4 years...", not "Structured technical program manager..."). This overrides the "[soft skill] [title]" opener still written in ideal-candidate, cv-pass-gap-fix and humanizer-us-resume.
- Profile: summarize experience only, no metrics or achievement numbers. Avoid overused resume terms (e.g. "detail-oriented"); when the JD names attention to detail, use a fresher phrase like "a sharp eye for detail".
- Profile years: a plain whole number, no hedges like "nearly". Don't imply all the years were in the target title when some were in another function.
- The title in the headline under the name and the title in the Profile must match.
- Bullets: don't repeat the same opening verb across experiences. Internships use weaker verbs ("managed", not "led").
- Experience entries ordered most recent first.
- Skills: label the last line of tail items "Others:", not "Also:".
- Every education entry carries dates; date ranges use an en dash ("Jun 2025 – Sep 2025"), not "to".
- When the job location differs from the candidate's location, put "(able to relocate)" right after the location in the header — unless the candidate's profile states "Willing to relocate: No", in which case never add it (see Screening and checks below for what that also means for whether the job is suitable at all).
- Ideal-candidate versions stay credible: don't track the JD wording too closely, no unrealistic figures.

## Screening and checks
- Relocation gate: default to treating relocation as possible (location gate passes) when the profile doesn't say otherwise. But if the profile explicitly states "Willing to relocate: No", that overrides any "open to anywhere" location-preference text — a job that's neither remote, nor in the candidate's current city, nor in their stated desired city/location is not suitable and should fail the location gate (don't add "(able to relocate)" and don't soften this into a pass). Remote jobs always pass regardless of relocation willingness. Count x.5 years as x+1 against a minimum years requirement.
- Employer-side state/region hiring exclusions (e.g. "we do not hire remote employees in MT, LA, ..." for state-tax reasons) are a different check from the relocation gate above, not a variant of it — this is the employer refusing to hire in the candidate's current state at all, remote or not, regardless of the candidate's own relocation preference. Drop (don't just flag) a posting that names an explicit exclusion covering the candidate's current state or region, unless the user says otherwise for that run.
- National-security / nationality gate (added 2026-10-04): drop a posting outright, at sourcing time, if it's a national-security/defense/intelligence-domain role (explicit security clearance requirement, or company/role framing around national security, defense contracting, classified work, ITAR, etc.) or otherwise gates on a specific nationality or citizenship the candidate's profile doesn't show them holding. Don't wait for `ideal-cv-pipeline-v4`'s JD analysis to catch this as a handoff later, the way the AeroVironment/clearance case did — apply it as its own sourcing-stage check, same spirit as the relocation and state-exclusion gates above, even though `candidate-job-matcher`/`candidate-job-matcher-hirebase`'s own title/seniority/YOE filters can't see it on their own.
- YOE floor, capped: when checking a posting's stated minimum-years requirement against a candidate's own computed YOE, the normal floor is the candidate's own YOE minus 1. Once the candidate has 6 or more years of experience, that floor caps at 4 and does not climb any higher as the candidate's YOE grows further — a candidate with 7, 10, or 15 years still only needs a posting to state "4+ years" or more to clear the floor, not something closer to their own actual tenure. Below 6 years of candidate YOE, the floor stays candidate YOE minus 1, uncapped. The ceiling (never count a posting asking for more years than the candidate actually has) is unchanged either way.
- Score strictly and consistently: skills weigh most (a missing core skill can mean an immediate fail), then experience, then profile and title, education last even when the JD lists a degree.
- Framework/platform-depth gate (added 2026-10-06): the sourcing-stage stack-match gate deliberately only checks core implementation language (a candidate with no Spring Boot but real Python/Go/Java isn't rejected for that alone — frameworks are learnable, a missing language isn't the same gap). That leaves a hard-stated named framework, a specific cloud platform with production-years attached (e.g. "5+ years hands-on production AWS/Azure/GCP, including Kubernetes"), or a skill-*composition*-specific years requirement (e.g. "7+ years of full-stack experience," narrower than the candidate's total YOE) with zero profile evidence to slip through sourcing uncaught. `ideal-cv-pipeline-v4`'s own JD analysis used to catch this incidentally as a stage 2a handoff — but that step doesn't run at all when resume generation uses a non-local path (e.g. ElasticDash-BE's own `regenerate-resume` endpoint), so skipping local generation silently drops this check too. Run a dedicated pass/fail JD-fit read (see `wideapply-campaign-pipeline`'s Stage 1.5) before any resume/lead work regardless of which resume-generation path is used — confirmed on a real run (candidate Mark Matas, 2026-10-06): two already-logged applications (Accenture, Allstate) both had this exact gap, and a systemic sweep of the rest of that sourcing round found 9 more. **A single posting can name the same technology multiple times with inconsistent framing — checking only the first or most obvious mention isn't enough** (confirmed on a later run, candidate Tarunn Gusain, 2026-10-07: a Nielsen FDE posting mentioned AWS four times, three of them safely OR'd or "preferred," but one separate bullet buried mid-posting stated a hard, standalone requirement for a named AWS service — AWS Cognito — with zero profile evidence; it was missed because the other three mentions were checked and found safe, and the posting was marked clear without separately verifying the remaining occurrence). Both `wideapply-campaign-pipeline`'s Stage 1.5 and `candidate-job-matcher-hirebase`'s own sourcing-stage stack-match check now say explicitly: scan every occurrence of each named technology in the posting independently, since one OR'd or soft mention elsewhere does not clear a different hard-framed mention of the same technology.
- cv-pass-check always ends with a clear final result of Pass or Fail at the bottom.
- When screening against a JD, give the assessment only. Don't offer outreach emails, LinkedIn messages or client-facing copy.

## This repo

Local Claude Code rebuild of the `ideal-cv-pipeline-v4` kit (originally built for claude.ai), plus `wideapply-campaign-pipeline`, which chains it with job sourcing and lead-to-todo conversion into a full campaign run. See `PROMPTS.md` for ready-to-send templates for every flow below.

- `ideal-cv-pipeline-v4` — JD + CV → one-page resume → Drive push → leads. `/ideal-cv-pipeline-v4` or "跑一遍 pipeline". Settings: `IDEAL_CANDIDATE`, `AUTO_FIX_ROUNDS`, `PUSH_TO_DRIVE`, `PUSH_LEADS`. (Standalone use only — see next bullet for why `wideapply-campaign-pipeline` turns `PUSH_TO_DRIVE` off.)
- `wideapply-campaign-pipeline` — `campaignId` in → applications sourced → resume per application → finalized against that application → leads converted to connect-todos. Fully automatic past one checkpoint (the sourced-jobs go-ahead). Settings: `TARGET_APPLICATION_COUNT`, `ATTACH_TO_APPLICATION`, `CONVERT_LEADS_TO_TODOS`, `BACKFILL_HANDOFFS`, `MAX_BACKFILL_ROUNDS`.
- **`TARGET_APPLICATION_COUNT` means N finished outcomes, not N sourcing attempts (fixed 2026-10-03).** A real run asking for 3 applications sourced 3, but 2 hit handoff (BIM/AEC domain gap, missing DoD clearance) — gaps `candidate-job-matcher`'s own title/seniority/YOE filters can't see, since they only surface during `ideal-cv-pipeline-v4`'s JD/candidate analysis, after the sourcing slot is already spent. Added Stage 3 (`BACKFILL_HANDOFFS`, default on): when a handoff or thin-JD skip leaves the finished count under target, source exactly that many replacements and run stage 2 on just the new ones, looping up to `MAX_BACKFILL_ROUNDS` (default 3) rounds or until `candidate-job-matcher` reports its search genuinely exhausted. Relies on that skill's existing seen-jobs dedup cache to avoid re-offering the same rejected postings.
- **Resumes go to S3, not Google Drive, for this pipeline (changed 2026-10-02).** `push-resume-to-application` uploads straight to ElasticDash-BE's own S3-backed storage (real multipart, the same path `uploadBuffer`/`controller/general/file.js` already used reliably), replacing the claude.ai Google Drive connector (`mcp__claude_ai_Google_Drive__*`, base64), which hit a reproducible corruption bug (confirmed: one resume silently truncated from 223KB to 18KB, twice) and needed a font-stripped workaround on another run. `wideapply-campaign-pipeline` sets `PUSH_TO_DRIVE = false` on its per-application `ideal-cv-pipeline-v4` calls accordingly — `push-resume-to-drive` is still fine for one-off standalone resume delivery outside this pipeline, just not used here anymore.
- **One resume now produces two separate application documents, split by audience, not just format (settled 2026-10-02).** `wideapply.ApplicationDocuments.kind='cv'` is now specifically the **candidate-facing** file and must be a PDF/image — `wideapply-dashboard`'s `DocumentTab.tsx` only previews those inline, and a `.docx` there renders neither a preview nor a download link (an earlier "two `cv` rows, one per format" design was tried and walked back for exactly this reason: the candidate could end up seeing an empty tab, which defeats the whole point). The new `kind='cv_source'` (migration 058) holds the **admin-only** `.docx` — `applicationDocumentController.js`'s admin upload path (`createWideapplyDocument`) accepts it, `boardController.js`'s candidate-facing query (`getWideapplyApplicationDetailForUser`) explicitly excludes it so it never reaches a candidate's browser at all, and `createWideapplyDocumentForUser` (the candidate's own self-service replace) deliberately does **not** accept it. `drive_review_url` (migration 057, admin-only) defaults to the `.docx`/`cv_source` upload's `fileUrl` if one was given, else the PDF's. Each kind replaces only a document of the same kind — `cv` and `cv_source` never collide.
- `ElasticDash-BE`'s own admin panel (`wideapply-dashboard`, a separate repo) already renders every non-screenshot document (not just one), labeled by kind (`ApplicationDetailAdminModal.tsx`'s `DOCUMENT_KIND_LABEL`), each with a `FilePreviewButton` (preview+download for PDF/image, direct download link otherwise) — added 2026-10-02, since admins need to actually open/verify what got uploaded. The optimistic local-state replace logic in `DocumentTab.tsx`, `ApplicationDrawer.tsx` and the admin modal (`onDocumentReplaced`/`onFileSelected`) matches a prior same-kind document by file format too, not just kind — a holdover from an earlier "two `cv` rows, same kind, different format" design that was walked back (see bullet above), left in place because it's harmless: with one format per kind now, format-aware and kind-only matching behave identically, so there's no reason to churn it back.
- A `wideapply.CampaignApplications` row's resume-related admin field is `drive_review_url` (migration 057), separate from its `wideapply.ApplicationDocuments` files (migration 026/058, see bullet above). Never add `drive_review_url` or a `cv_source` document to `boardController.js`'s candidate-facing path.
- `recruiter-intro-email` (added 2026-10-05) is a standalone content skill — writes the one-off "I just applied" email a candidate sends a recruiter/hiring manager, from resume + JD + posting URL + recipient info, no API calls. Not part of any pipeline here. Its lens/template (10-second-scan test, P1/P2/P3/closer structure, 120-200 word cap, hyperlinked job title, mandatory "Hi [First Name]," recipient greeting) is also what `convertWideapplyLeadToEmailSequence`'s Email 1 in ElasticDash-BE (`controller/wideapply/emailSequenceGenerator.js`) was rewritten to match — the two should be kept in sync if either's rules change, since they're now describing the same email from two different places (a standalone drafting skill here vs. the automated Day 0 draft that skill produces there).
- Every ElasticDash-BE write from this repo (`push-wideapply-leads`, `push-wideapply-applications`, `push-resume-to-application`, `convert-wideapply-lead-to-todo`, `convert-wideapply-lead-to-email`, `push-wideapply-resume`) needs a `campaignId`/`applicationId`/`leadId` given by the user — never guessed or looked up — and its own scoped service key under a separate `.secrets/wideapply-*-skill/.env.<env>` (see each skill's "One-time setup"). All six of these skills now live in this repo's own `.claude/skills/`, not globally — run their scripts from the repo root (`node .claude/skills/<skill>/<script>.mjs ...`).
- `convert-wideapply-lead-to-email` (added 2026-10-03) drafts a 3-email Day 0/3/6 outreach sequence from a lead with an email address — approval-gated, never auto-sends. Deliberately **not** wired into `wideapply-campaign-pipeline`'s own stage 2c (the skill file's default stays LinkedIn-only, unlike its LinkedIn sibling) — the automatic wiring is instead opt-in at the prompt layer: `PROMPTS.md` template 9 chains it per eligible lead explicitly, as a deliberate choice for that one named flow, without changing the orchestrator skill or template 7's unchanged behavior. It also requires the target application to already have an `email_sender_id` assigned from its candidate's sender pool — a separate, still entirely manual admin step with no skill of its own yet.

- `convert-wideapply-lead-to-todo` takes two optional fields now (CLI: `--message`/`--follow-up-message`, body: `message`/`followUpMessage`): `message` (added 2026-10-03) is a pre-drafted LinkedIn connect note saved as the primary to-do's content (sent *with* the connection request); `followUpMessage` (added 2026-10-03) is a pre-drafted note for the chain's own "Send private message to `<name>`" step (sent *after* the connection is accepted — see `createLinkedinConnectionCheckChain` in `adminController.js`). Both threaded through `leadsController.js`'s `convertWideapplyLeadToTodo` and the `/leads/:leadId/convert` route. `PROMPTS.md` template 9 is the first caller that drafts and passes `followUpMessage`; every other caller (templates 1-7, the admin panel) still gets the old empty-content behavior by omitting both.

- **`push-wideapply-resume` (added 2026-10-04) closes the "candidate has no parsed resume yet" gap** that blocked every `convert-wideapply-lead-to-email` attempt on real runs (confirmed: 0/8 attempted on a DraftKings run for candidate Agrima Jain, campaign `59a71ceb-4de2-4849-83a0-d4f9a7ce37f0`). `wideapply.Resumes.raw_text` (candidate-scoped, keyed by `ed_user_id`) is a completely separate store from `wideapply.ApplicationDocuments` (per-application, no text-extraction step) — attaching a resume to every application via `push-resume-to-application` never touches it. The skill resolves `edUserId` from the given `campaignId` (`GET /admin/campaigns/:campaignId`), uploads the `.docx` (`POST /resumes/upload`), extracts plain text locally by unzipping `word/document.xml` (no backend text-extraction step exists on this route — don't confuse it with `POST /wideapply/resume/parse`, a stateless onboarding-side LLM extraction that never writes to this table), then `PATCH`es `raw_text`/`parse_status: 'parsed'` onto the row. One-time per candidate, not per application. Verified against the live dev backend 2026-10-04: parsed Agrima Jain's resume (3243 chars), then successfully drafted a real email sequence for a DraftKings manager lead that had previously 400'd with this exact error.
- `.secrets/` is gitignored wholesale (see `.gitignore`) — everything needed to run these skills lives inside this repo, but nothing in that one directory ever gets committed.
- **`candidate-job-matcher-hirebase` (added 2026-10-04) is a Hirebase-backed alternative to the global `candidate-job-matcher` skill** — same seniority/YOE/title-expansion judgment, different job-search provider. Lives in this repo's own `.claude/skills/` (not globally, unlike `candidate-job-matcher`) because Hirebase has no MCP server: a local script, `hirebase-search.mjs`, calls Hirebase's REST API directly, reading `HIREBASE_API_KEY` straight from the repo-root `.env` (not `.secrets/` — this is a read-only external search, not an ElasticDash-BE write). Key differences from the TheirStack version worth remembering: Hirebase parses years-of-experience server-side into a `yoe`/`yoe_range` filter, eliminating candidate-job-matcher's client-side regex pass for the single-dimension case (the multi-dimensional case — e.g. separate dev-years and management-years requirements in one posting — still needs a manual description read, since `yoe_range` is only one parsed range); `geo_locations` takes plain city/region/country strings with no catalog-ID lookup step; and Hirebase has no server-side exclude-by-id or discovered-since filter, so repeat-run dedup is client-side only and costs a credit per re-pulled job (Hirebase bills per job *returned*), unlike TheirStack's `discovered_at_gte` which excluded already-seen jobs before they were ever charged. **Rewired 2026-10-04**: both `push-wideapply-applications` and `wideapply-campaign-pipeline` now take a `JOB_SOURCE` setting (`"hirebase"`, new default, or `"theirstack"`) instead of calling `candidate-job-matcher` by name. `push-wideapply-applications`'s Step 3 mapping table was split into a Hirebase branch and a TheirStack branch (different result field names, different `source` value, and Hirebase's `description` needs `return_raw_description: "true"` requested or it arrives truncated to ~900 chars, which is thin enough to trip `MIN_JOB_DESCRIPTION_CHARS` downstream). The two providers keep separate seen-jobs dedup caches — don't switch `JOB_SOURCE` mid-run (e.g. between a Stage 1 search and a Stage 3 backfill round in `wideapply-campaign-pipeline`), since that loses the accumulated dedup history for that candidate.
- **Lead outreach content (LinkedIn message + email sequence) is entirely backend-generated now, not drafted by this repo (changed 2026-10-05).** Previously `wideapply-campaign-pipeline`'s `bulk_import` mode and `PROMPTS.md` template 9 had Claude draft per-tier LinkedIn messages (passed via `convert-wideapply-lead-to-todo`'s `--message`/`--follow-up-message`) and relied on TinyFish company research to fill in the templates. That drafting step is gone — ElasticDash-BE's `emailSequenceGenerator.js` (`generateWideapplyEmailSequence`) and `leadMessageGenerator.js` (`generateWideapplyLeadMessage`/`generateWideapplyLeadFollowUpMessages`) own all of it now. Email was already fully backend-generated at conversion time (`convertWideapplyLeadToEmailSequence` calls the generator itself — nothing to change there). LinkedIn wasn't: `convertWideapplyLeadToTodo` only ever stored whatever content a caller passed in, defaulting to empty, and the backend's own generation only fired later off the candidate's own lifecycle (accept-confirmation, then a 3-day reply check). `convert-wideapply-lead-to-todo` gained a second script, `generate-message.mjs` (calls the admin `regenerate-message` route), to trigger that same generation immediately after conversion instead of waiting — every caller of that skill (both pipeline modes, `PROMPTS.md` templates 6 and 9) now runs it right after every convert call. TinyFish company/product research didn't disappear, it moved: write it into each lead's `notes` field at `push-wideapply-leads` time instead of into a drafted message, since `generateWideapplyLeadMessage` already reads that field back as `leadNotes`.
- **Fixed a real gap this surfaced: `regenerateWideapplyLeadMessage` (the function behind `/regenerate-message`, so also behind `generate-message.mjs` above) wasn't passing `tier`, `jobDescription`, or `leadNotes` to the generator at all (fixed 2026-10-05, ElasticDash-BE `controller/wideapply/leadsController.js`)** — only `publishGeneratedLeadMessage` (the automatic accept-confirmation path in `campaignsController.js`) passed that context before this fix, so every message generated via the admin-triggered route fell back to the generic manager/peer hook regardless of the lead's actual tier. Now both paths pass the same context.
- **LinkedIn goes to every lead tier; email is gated to Manager and `hr_recruiting` only — enforced on the backend already, nothing to add here.** `convertWideapplyLeadToTodo` has no tier check at all. `convertWideapplyLeadToEmailSequence` hard-rejects (400) any tier outside `EMAIL_SEQUENCE_ELIGIBLE_TIERS = ['manager', 'hr_recruiting']`. Getting a lead's `tier` right at `push-wideapply-leads` time (see `hiring-contact-finder`'s own bucket→tier mapping, now just `manager`→`manager`/`hr_recruiting`→`hr_recruiting` as of 2026-10-06) is now load-bearing for both the backend's message tone and this email gate, not just a label.
- **`candidate-job-matcher-hirebase`'s real pulls now sort by `relevance`, not `date_posted`, with `keywords` always carrying the candidate's full skills list (changed 2026-10-05, user directive).** Per Hirebase's own docs, `keywords` is a ranking signal only (exact phrase > all-words > any-words match, OR'd, never narrows `total_count` to zero) — it was previously treated as an optional add-on and the first filter stripped on a thin pull, which meant it had no effect on page ordering anyway, since the real pulls were hardcoded to `sort_by: "date_posted"`. Switching both together makes skill-matching jobs cluster on page 1 instead of requiring a second-page pull to find them. Trade-off: freshest-first ordering within a page is gone (a relevance-sorted page mixes job ages), but the absolute staleness ceiling is untouched — `days_ago` (7, widening to 14, hard-capped at 14) still bounds every pull to the same fresh window as before, this only changes ordering *within* that window. The old `lastSeenDatePosted` soft freshness-bias trick is dropped (it only made sense against a date-sorted page); client-side `_id` dedup is unaffected and remains the only real dedup mechanism.
- **`fetch-ats-jobs.mjs` (added 2026-10-05, `.claude/skills/fetch-ats-jobs/`) pulls postings within the last N days (default 7) directly from the JSON APIs behind `.temp/grokbot/sources.csv`'s Greenhouse/Ashby/Lever/Workday `kind=company` rows**, instead of the TinyFish/browser investigation that CSV otherwise records per company. Greenhouse/Ashby/Lever each return the full job list plus description in one call, with a real posted-date field (`first_published`/`publishedAt`/`createdAt`); Workday has no such single call (results page at a hard `limit=20`, with only a relative `postedOn` string like "Posted 3 Days Ago" on the list endpoint) but is sorted newest-first, so pagination stops as soon as a page goes stale, and only in-window candidates get the extra per-job detail call for an exact `startDate` and full description. `kind=board` rows (Work at a Startup, Wellfound, a16z, Built In, HiringCafe, ...) and `platform=custom` rows (Rippling, DocuSign, Yelp, Gem) are skipped and reported, not silently dropped — neither exposes a uniform per-company JSON API. Verified on the repo's real `sources.csv` 2026-10-05: 94/107 rows fetched, 1,319 jobs within 7 days, in about 4 minutes. Writes per-company JSON files plus `all_jobs.json`/`_summary.json`/`_summary.csv` to `.temp/grokbot/fetched_jobs/` (gitignored, like the rest of `.temp/`).
- **A `fetch-ats-jobs.mjs` run is meant to feed `wideapply-campaign-pipeline`'s `MODE = "bulk_import"` directly (wired 2026-10-06), with two defaults that differ from the GrokBot-markdown use of that same mode**: `APPLICATIONS_PER_DAY = 4` instead of 20, and the schedule's start date defaults to the coming Monday (the first day of the calendar week *after* today), weekdays only — not today, and not including weekends, the way the GrokBot flow's calendar-day pacing still works unchanged. Rationale: this source refreshes weekly at a 7-day freshness window, so it's meant to pace a normal week of applying (4/day × 5 weekdays = 20), not front-load whatever's left of the current week or spend a large one-time batch. See `wideapply-campaign-pipeline` Stage 0 steps 1/5/7 for the exact field mapping (`fetch-ats-jobs.mjs`'s already-normalized JSON fields map straight across, no markdown-table guessing) and `PROMPTS.md` template 13 for the ready-to-send chained prompt. Leads and their connect-todos/email sequences are **not** a separate backend-side schedule (no due-date column exists on those tables) — they land on whatever real day their application's `apply_date` batch is actually processed, so the weekly pacing only holds together if the daily batches really run daily (the `schedule` skill's cron), not all in one sitting.
- **`hiring-contact-finder`'s own default dropped from 4 buckets/20 leads to 2 buckets/5 leads per company (changed 2026-10-06, user directive): Manager (up to 3) and HR/Recruiting (up to 2) only, C-level and Colleague are no longer fetched at all.** This is a global skill (`~/.claude/skills/hiring-contact-finder/`, not scoped to this repo), edited directly rather than overridden at the call site, matching how this same skill's quotas were already changed in place once before (2026-10-03, see `PROMPTS.md` template 9's history) — callers in this repo (`ideal-cv-pipeline-v4` stage 9, `wideapply-campaign-pipeline`'s 2c, `PROMPTS.md` templates 1/9) just inherit the new default, none of them hardcode the old quotas. `push-wideapply-leads` and the backend's `tier` enum still accept `c_level`/`peer` for leads sourced some other way; this skill simply stops producing either.
- **Job-title expansion is now a fixed lookup table, not a per-run "generate 3-6 variants" judgment call (changed 2026-10-06, user directive).** All three matcher skills (`candidate-job-matcher`, `candidate-job-matcher-hirebase`, `candidate-job-matcher-grokbot`) used to regenerate title variants fresh every run with no fixed synonym table — confirmed causing real leaks on actual runs (`Intertwine-AI-Data-Engineer-MidLevel` and `DraftKings-Senior-ML-Engineer-FinTech` sourced under a general Software Engineer search). The canonical table now lives in `candidate-job-matcher`'s SKILL.md Step 2 (the other two skills already deferred to its Step 2 by reference, so they just point at the new table instead of generating independently): **Software Engineer** family (base nouns Software Engineer/Developer/Programmer/SWE/SDE/bare Engineer, plus a 4-way specialization containment — Full Stack reaches outward to Frontend/Backend/generic, but Frontend and Backend are each isolated and do NOT match Full Stack or each other, Mobile is fully isolated too, matching only Mobile+generic), a disambiguation exclusion list (Data/ML/QA/Security/Network/DevOps-SRE-Platform/Mechanical-etc./Sales-Solutions-Support Engineer are different fields, not SWE specializations), and a skill-gated exception for Game/Graphics Engineer (excluded unless the candidate's Skills list shows a game-specific skill like Unity/Unreal/C++/OpenGL). Plus **Program/Project Manager** (PM, TPM — treated as full synonyms, not a directional containment, since they share the same responsibility; bare "PM" deliberately excluded from the inclusion list, too overloaded with Product Manager), **Product Manager** (the mirror disambiguation partner), and **Data Scientist/Analyst** (full synonyms, Business Analyst as a looser variant, excludes Data Engineer/ML Engineer/generic SWE). A title family not in the table yet (Designer, Sales, Marketing, ...) falls back to the old judgment call, stated explicitly as a fallback rather than silently guessed. `candidate-job-matcher-grokbot`'s output JSON gained a new `titles.excludeOffDomain` field for the disambiguation list (its schema previously only had `excludeTooSenior`/`excludeTooJunior`). Full design history and the open questions this was checked against: `.temp/title-matching-rules-draft.md`.
