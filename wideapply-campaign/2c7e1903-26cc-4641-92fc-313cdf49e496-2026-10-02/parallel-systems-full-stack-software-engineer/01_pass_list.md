# Pass List: Full Stack Software Engineer (Software Engineer I) — Parallel Systems

This is a junior "Software Engineer I" role dressed up as "Full Stack Software Engineer," on an Interfaces team that builds operator software for a physical vehicle fleet. What makes it different from a generic junior full-stack posting is the Rust + Electron + hardware-adjacent stack, and the explicit framing that ownership and learning speed matter more than years.

## HR pass list

| # | Check | Where | What passes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Work authorization | Form | Authorized to work in the US without sponsorship, or sponsorship need disclosed and accepted |
| 2 | Location | Form | Based in or around Los Angeles, CA, or willing to relocate (onsite role; relocation always passes this check) |
| 3 | Degree | Skim | Bachelor's in CS, computer engineering, or related field, or equivalent practical experience shown via a bootcamp plus portfolio, research, or shipped projects |
| 4 | Years in titled roles | Skim | Not a gate; "0+ years" (JD explicitly does not require production experience) |
| 5 | Required stack keywords | Skim | At least two of TypeScript, React, Rust visible in Skills or project descriptions; Python counts as a partial credit per "Rust, React, TypeScript, or Python" |
| 6 | Distinguishing requirement as a keyword | Skim | "Rust" or "Electron" visible somewhere on the CV, tied to the hardware/vehicle/operator-software framing of the role |
| 7 | Overqualification | Skim | Candidate reads as junior/early-career (0-2 years); a candidate presenting as senior (staff/lead titles, 5+ years at the same level) is likely filtered against this narrow, junior-pegged $122k-$141k band |

Notes: Visa sponsorship is not addressed in the JD text given; confirm on the live posting since vehicle-fleet/robotics employers sometimes carry export-control knockouts. The degree line is unusually generous ("bootcamp plus portfolio, research, or shipped projects are fine") but HR will still look for a degree line or an explicit portfolio substitute; a CV with neither is a soft miss at skim.

## Hiring manager pass list

| # | Check | What passes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Shipped TypeScript/React (or equivalent component UI) work | A named project or role where the candidate built real UI in TypeScript and a component framework (React or similar), not a Skills-list mention |
| 2 | Fast pickup of an unfamiliar language or tool | A specific instance of becoming productive in a new language/stack quickly (class project, internship, research, open source, or something self-initiated), evidence of speed and follow-through, not just a list of languages |
| 3 | End-to-end feature ownership | A bullet or narrative showing the full cycle: understanding the user/requirement, implementing, testing, documenting, and following through after merge, not just "built X feature" |
| 4 | Collaboration with non-software stakeholders to define interfaces | Evidence of working directly with product, operations, hardware, or another non-engineering counterpart to turn ambiguous operational needs into a working interface |

**Likely fifth (borderline)**: Backend/API or data-model exposure (FastAPI, Node.js/NestJS, database work). Most hiring managers will waive this if checks 1-4 are strong, since the JD frames it as something the candidate "grows into," not a day-one requirement.

Soft skills from the required section (high agency, clear communication, hunger to learn fast, honesty about AI tool mistakes) sit on neither list; a CV cannot prove them, they are tested in interview.

## Not needed to pass either list

Desktop wrapper experience beyond a baseline mention (Electron/Tauri specifics), explicit backend/database depth, coursework in networks/OS/distributed systems, and any advanced degree or certification are preferred-only and do not gate either screen.

## Ranking among passes

Among candidates who pass both gates, rank first by direct Electron/desktop-wrapper experience (N1), since it is the one piece of the stack most specific to this product (an Electron operator app) and closest to the company's core business; second by backend/API/database depth (N2), since the role explicitly grows into full-stack work with data models and APIs; third by systems coursework or projects (networks, OS, distributed systems, N3), which signals readiness for the performance/reliability work mentioned in the 90-day plan but is the furthest from day-one responsibilities. A CV that also shows direct collaboration across a hardware/software boundary (vehicle, robotics, or other real-world-system teams) ranks above an otherwise-equal candidate from a purely web/SaaS background, since that mirrors the "vehicle/systems engineers" collaboration named in the JD.
