# Pass Lists: Software Engineer (Backend) — Cardless

A deliberately general backend role at a card-issuing fintech, open mid-level through staff, where the distinguishing requirement is comfort operating in a regulated, money-moving system rather than any single required tech stack.

## HR pass list

| # | Check | Where | What passes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Work authorization / sponsorship | Form | Answer on application (JD does not state visa policy) |
| 2 | Location: San Francisco onsite | Form | Based in SF/Bay Area, or willing to relocate |
| 3 | Years in backend engineering roles | Skim | 2+ years (mid-level floor implied by "mid-level through staff") in roles titled Software Engineer, Backend Engineer, or equivalent engineering title |
| 4 | Backend language/ecosystem keyword | Skim | Java, or another modern backend language (Python, Go, Node.js, etc.) visible in a production-engineering context |
| 5 | Distinguishing requirement as keyword | Skim | "Payments", "fintech", "lending", or "card issuing" (or a regulated-industry equivalent such as healthcare/compliance) visible in company or role description |
| 6 | Salary expectation within band | Form | Expectation within $160k-$295k |

Notes: the pay band is wide and spans mid-level to staff, so there is no real overqualification filter here. The JD names no degree requirement, so no degree knockout applies unless the live posting's application questions add one.

## Hiring manager pass list

| # | Check | What passes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Production backend ownership end to end | Named a specific backend service/project they scoped, designed, implemented, tested, and rolled out to production, not just a prototype |
| 2 | Distributed systems / API / database fundamentals in practice | Built or operated APIs and a data layer (SQL/NoSQL) as part of a real system, not a Skills-list mention |
| 3 | Operating in a regulated or high-correctness environment | Worked directly with compliance, risk, clinical, or similarly scrutinized stakeholders on a system where correctness mattered |
| 4 | Post-launch operational ownership | Evidence of monitoring, observability, or iterating on a system after it shipped, not just the build phase |

**Likely fifth (borderline)**: payments/fintech domain experience specifically (authorization, ledgers, settlement, reconciliation, disputes). Most managers will waive this given the JD's explicit statement that they'd rather hire someone sharp who learns the domain, as long as checks 1-4 are strong.

Soft skills from the required section (systems thinking on unfamiliar systems, clear communication, genuine care about correctness) sit on neither list; a CV cannot prove them and they are tested in interview.

## Not needed to pass either list

Workflow orchestration (Temporal, Step Functions) or event streaming experience, external partner-facing API experience specifically, and any advanced degree or certification are all preferred/ranking items, not gates.

## Ranking among passes

Among candidates who pass both gates, direct payments/lending/card-issuing experience (authorization, ledgers, settlement, reconciliation, disputes, regulatory work) ranks first since it is the company's core business and the JD says it is weighted heavily. Next, experience building and maintaining external, partner-facing APIs ranks highly since partner integration is central to Cardless's product. Workflow orchestration or event streaming experience (Temporal, Step Functions) ranks after that as a tooling plus. Responsibility items that did not make the hiring-manager pass list but still separate candidates: working across multiple domains/services rather than owning a single narrow service, and direct collaboration with partner/vendor counterparts (not just internal stakeholders).
