# Pass List — Senior AI Platform Engineer, Hasbro / Wizards of the Coast

An internal AI/developer-platform tooling role carrying a "Senior" title over a surprisingly low stated floor (2+ years, "or equivalent experience"); the real differentiator is production LLM/agentic delivery plus building tools other engineers or non-technical staff actually use, not raw tenure.

## HR pass list

| # | Check | Where | What passes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Work authorization | Form | Authorized to work in the US without sponsorship |
| 2 | Location | Form | Based in Renton, WA / greater Seattle area, or willing to relocate |
| 3 | Years in titled roles | Skim | 2+ years (1.5 is fine under x.5 rounding) in roles titled Software Engineer, AI Engineer, ML Engineer, Developer, or equivalent doing cloud/production development |
| 4 | Required tools | Skim | At least 2 of Python, TypeScript, CDK, SAP, GitHub, GitLab, AWS visible in work experience, not only a Skills list |
| 5 | LLM/AI keyword | Skim | "Prompt engineering" and/or "agent" / "agentic" visible in a real project, since this is the distinguishing requirement for an "AI" title |
| 6 | SDLC keyword | Skim | CI/CD, Git-based workflow, or similar modern SDLC language visible |
| 7 | Seniority/title match | Skim | A recruiter screening against a "Senior" title looks for a prior "Senior" or equivalent lead title, or at minimum 4-5+ years of continuous relevant experience to substitute for it |

Notes:
- The JD's stated years floor (2+) is low relative to the "Senior" title; a strict recruiter may still informally expect senior-sounding titles or 5+ years given the title, even though the text does not require it. This is the main soft risk, not a hard knockout in the text itself.
- No degree requirement is stated; an advanced CS degree is a plus at the skim but not a gate.

## Hiring manager pass list

| # | Check | What passes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Production LLM/agentic delivery | Built and deployed a prompt-engineered or agent-based LLM system into production (not a prototype/demo), with a named outcome |
| 2 | Internal developer-platform/tooling ownership | Built an internal API, SDK, tool, or platform feature that other engineers or non-technical internal users adopted |
| 3 | Enterprise automation / SaaS integration | Automated a manual process or integrated multiple systems for an enterprise/internal customer using AI |
| 4 | Cloud production deployment discipline | Owned deployment of a production cloud service (AWS or similar) with monitoring/observability, not just local or notebook work |

**Likely fifth (borderline)**: Low-code/no-code tooling for non-developers (H3) - most hiring managers will waive this if checks 1-4 are strong, since it is one responsibility line among several rather than a named must-have.

Soft skills from the required section (cross-functional collaboration, driving adoption without mandate, versatility) sit on neither list; they are tested in interview.

## Not needed to pass either list

AWS Bedrock/SageMaker AI experience, cloud account/settings configuration experience, and any advanced degree are preferred-only; none are required to pass HR or hiring-manager gates.

## Ranking among passes

Among candidates who pass both gates, AWS Bedrock or SageMaker AI experience ranks first since it is the closest nice-to-have to the role's actual agentic-AI work; enterprise AI/automation deployment experience ranks second; cloud account/architecture configuration experience ranks third as a more general infra skill. A candidate whose internal-platform work shipped to large numbers of developer users, versus a single small-team tool, separates further within the pass group.
